The source skills (`cms-claims`, `cms-provider-data`, `nppes`, `open-payments`, `openfda`, `census`) each document one API. This skill covers how to combine them without overstating what the combined number means. If an `hcupnet-query` skill is available, it covers all-payer *inpatient* hospital data (AHRQ HCUP).

## Rule 1: keep measured and estimated figures separate

Every combined answer has two clearly labeled parts:

- **Measured:** numbers returned by a query, each with its source and period.
- **Estimated:** anything derived from an assumption (a Medicare Advantage adjustment, an all-payer scale-up, a growth projection). Show the formula, each input and its source, and the assumption in words.

Never fold an estimate into a measured total. Never present a modeled number with more precision than its weakest input; round it and say "approximately".

## Joining sources

| Join key | Connects |
|---|---|
| **NPI** (10 digits) | claims (`Rndrng_NPI`, `Rfrg_NPI`) ↔ NPPES ↔ Open Payments (`covered_recipient_npi`) ↔ clinician enrollment/affiliations (`npi`) ↔ ASC list (`npi`) |
| **CCN** (6 chars, hospitals) | inpatient/outpatient claims (`Rndrng_Prvdr_CCN`) ↔ Care Compare (`facility_id`) ↔ facility affiliations ↔ Open Payments teaching hospitals (`teaching_hospital_ccn`) |
| **County FIPS** (5 digits) | Medicare enrollment (`BENE_FIPS_CD`) ↔ Census (`state` + `county`) |
| **Company name** | Open Payments ↔ openFDA. **No shared ID.** Names differ in punctuation, legal suffix and subsidiary ("Smith+Nephew, Inc." vs "Smith & Nephew, Inc."; Howmedica for Stryker). List every variant matched, and say the match is name-based. |

Batch NPI lookups (`--in npi=...`, 50-100 per call) rather than looping one NPI at a time.

## Recipe: procedure market size

1. **Measured Medicare fee-for-service (FFS) volume.** Use `cms-claims`: `query geography` national (or state) rows for the code family.
   - Procedures ≈ `Tot_Benes` summed across place of service, not `Tot_Srvcs`. Assistant and facility lines inflate services.
   - Pull multiple years for trend; flag 2020 as a COVID dip.
2. **Site-of-care split (measured, partial views):**
   - Hospital outpatient department: `query outGeo` HCPCS rows.
   - Inpatient: `query inpGeo` for the matching MS-DRG(s). DRGs mix procedures, so map carefully and say so.
   - ASC: physician-file rows billed by ASC entities (`Rndrng_Prvdr_Type` = "Ambulatory Surgical Center" in `provider`).

   These come from different files with different counting units. Present them as a triangulation, not as parts that must sum exactly to step 1.
3. **Medicare Advantage-inclusive estimate.** Use `ffs-share` for the same geography, with `--year` set to the claims year (the default is the latest full year).
   - Estimate = FFS volume ÷ `ffs_share`.
   - The assumption: Medicare Advantage members have the procedure at the same rate as fee-for-service members. That's unproven; MA utilization management can lower rates. Label the result as an estimate.
   - Enrollment has years the claims data doesn't, and vice versa. Use the matching year when it exists, and say so when it doesn't.
4. **All-payer.** No free API covers commercial or Medicaid outpatient volume. Don't extrapolate one from Medicare.
   - Say it's out of scope, or use HCUP for inpatient all-payer (via `hcupnet-query` if available).
   - Note that inpatient-only data undercounts procedures that have moved outpatient. Total knee and hip replacement left Medicare's inpatient-only list in 2018 and 2020.
5. **Per-capita rates: match the numerator to the denominator.** Fee-for-service claims ÷ all residents 65+ understates the rate by roughly the Medicare Advantage share, because the denominator includes people whose procedures the numerator can't see. Report either:
   - **measured:** FFS procedures ÷ Original Medicare beneficiaries (`ffs-share`, same year); or
   - **estimated:** the Medicare Advantage-inclusive estimate ÷ population 65+ (`popest`).

   If you show the mismatched figure at all, label it "not comparable" and never lead with it.
6. **Value.** Volume × device ASP, where the ASP comes from the user. CMS data has no device prices; Medicare payment amounts are professional or facility payments, not device revenue.

## Recipe: account and surgeon targeting

1. Get the top billers for the code(s): `cms-claims` `query provider`, sorted by `Tot_Benes`. Filter by surgeon specialty, and drop `Rndrng_Prvdr_Ent_Cd=O` organizations and assistant-at-surgery specialties if you want operating surgeons.
2. Get where they operate: `cms-provider-data` `pd facilityAff` (hospitals) and `pd clinicians` (practice group; disambiguate multiple rows by address).
3. Get their industry relationships: `open-payments` `op recipient-entity --year Y --in covered_recipient_npi=<npi1>,<npi2>,...`, one call per batch of up to 100 NPIs.
4. Add hospital context: Care Compare ratings and `COMP_HIP_KNEE` complication rates (`cms-provider-data`). The complication window is multi-year, and its denominator is a measure cohort.
5. Check identity: NPPES (`nppes`) for current address, credential and taxonomy.

**Remember the redaction:** providers with ≤10 Medicare beneficiaries for a code are absent from by-provider files. A "top 100" list from claims omits low-volume Medicare surgeons and anyone treating mostly non-Medicare patients.

## Recipe: competitor intelligence

- **Products:** use `openfda` `510k`/`pma` by product code (find codes via `classification`), and count by `applicant.exact` to see who is active.
- **Safety signals:** `recall` / `enforcement` for recalls (check the recall class before calling one serious). Use MAUDE `event` counts only as "reported events", never as rates.
- **Physician relationships:** `open-payments` `entity-nature` (company totals by type) and `entity-recipient-nature` (which recipients). Royalty and consulting payments to high-volume surgeons are the usual KOL signal; payments are not product-use evidence.

## Compliance flags to raise

**Named clinician data**
- Open Payments, claims-by-NPI and NPPES identify individual clinicians. Using them for targeting is common and lawful, but how reps use payment information in conversations is a company-policy question. Say so when output is headed to a sales team.

**Small numbers**
- Don't re-identify patients from small counts or combine data in ways that would. The CMS files already suppress ≤10; don't try to back out suppressed values.

**Market-sizing outputs**
- Hand these over with the measured/estimated split intact, so the figure can't later be quoted as data.
